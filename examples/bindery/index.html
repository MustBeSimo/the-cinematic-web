I · The repair

We keep the binding the book already has.

Most bindings fail at the spine. The thread rots, the glue lets go, the cloth splits along the joint. We lift the old cloth, resew the text block on the stations it was first sewn on, line it in linen, and lay the original cloth back down. The book should look like itself, only able to open again.

  • Resewn on the original stations
  • Linen mull, wheat-starch paste
  • Spine cloth relaid, not replaced

II · On the bench

Books with a life already in them.

We take the books other binders send back: the ones that matter more than they are worth.

CLOTH · LEATHER

Bindings that split

Broken joints, detached boards, spines worn through at the head. Relined, reattached and consolidated, never rebacked in new material unless you ask.

BIBLES · LEDGERS

Heavy family books

Large text blocks with loose sections and handwritten pages. We do not trim, wash or flatten what someone wrote in the margins.

PAPERBACKS

The one read to pieces

Rebound in a plain cloth case with the original cover tipped in, so it can be read to pieces again.

III · Sending a book

Three steps, no cutting until you agree.

  1. Wrap it in clean paper, not plastic.Paper lets the book breathe in transit; plastic traps damp.
  2. Post it with a note about what it means to you.It changes what we keep. An inscription is not damage.
  3. We write back with a quote before any work begins.Photographs of the binding as it arrived, and what we propose to do.
